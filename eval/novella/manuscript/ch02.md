# Chapter Two — The Logbook

Lin spent the second day reading backward through the log, and the island reading her. She felt it — the cottage windows watched the village, and the village watched the cottage, and somewhere between them a decision was being made about her that she had no part in.

The log told a keeper's ordinary life: oil measured, wicks trimmed, weather noted in a dry hand. But threaded through the last year were the odd entries, always the same shape. *Kept it dark, as agreed.* Perhaps a dozen of them, never explained, each on a clear night when the lamp by every rule should have burned. And each, she noticed, falling near the new moon.

She was copying the dates into her own book when the light changed under the door — a shadow crossed the sill and stopped. By the time she reached it, whoever it was had gone into the dark that came off the sea. On the flagstone lay a square of paper, folded once, unsigned.

*You are keeping a dead man's appointment. Go back on the next boat, before the new moon, and you will be the first keeper of Halvis in a long while to leave it walking.*

The investigation, she understood then, was no longer about a man who had vanished. It was about an *arrangement* — one Aldous Vane had kept in his own hand for a year of new moons, one the island still expected the lighthouse to honour, one that someone wanted her gone before she could inherit. She turned the letter to the lamplight. The paper was good, deckle-edged, the kind you did not buy on Halvis.
