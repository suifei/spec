# Chapter Three — New Moon Minus One

The day before the new moon, Lin walked the shore road to the village and found it holding its breath. Doors that had been ajar closed as she passed. Only old Merrow, mending net on an upturned dory, would look at her, and only to say: "You read his log, then. You saw 'as agreed.' You going to ask me who he agreed *with*?"

"Who did he agree with?"

Merrow spat into the shingle. "Wrong question. Ask *what for*." And she would say no more, but her eyes went, once, to the north horn where the lighthouse stood — and then, deliberately, out to the black rocks of the Teeth, where in bad weather ships died and in good weather nothing at all should be.

Lin walked back as the light failed, and halfway along the shore road the last piece turned to face her.

*As agreed.* The log lied by telling the truth. Aldous had kept the lighthouse *dark* — because a dark lighthouse, on a moonless night, is an invitation. A boat that wants to come through the Teeth unseen needs the light off, needs the coast to trust the rocks are guarded when they are not. But the coast had seen a lamp the night he vanished. So the arrangement had two lights, not one: the lighthouse kept dark to hand the Teeth to the smugglers — and a second lamp, a lure hung out on the black rocks themselves, lit to bring the honest ships *onto* them. Wrecking. The warm brass she'd felt was innocent; it had not burned in weeks. The light the ferryman swore he'd seen had never been the lighthouse at all. It had been out on the Teeth, and someone had carried it there.
