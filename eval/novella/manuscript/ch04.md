# Chapter Four — The Deckle Edge

The paper told her where to look. Nobody on Halvis bought deckle-edged writing paper; but the schoolmistress on the mainland, who sold Lin her charts, kept a box of it for the one pupil who had ever gone from Halvis to a proper school and written home. That pupil had a name, and the name was Nore Vane.

Aldous had a daughter. She had left the island at fourteen and not come back — until, the schoolmistress said, six weeks ago, on the same tide that took Lin's posting to the office. Nore had come to bury a father nobody would admit was dead, and had stayed just long enough to slip a warning under a stranger's door.

Lin found her at the mainland inn, and Nore did not pretend. "I wrote it," she said. "I hoped you'd be a coward. Most keepers are — that's why they last. My father wasn't, at the end, and look." She turned her cup in its ring of damp. "I couldn't stop them keeping the light. I thought I could at least stop them needing another keeper. Stop *you*."

"They," said Lin. "You keep saying they."

"You've read the log. You know the shape of them. You just won't say the word either." Nore looked up, and for the first time she looked like her father's daughter — steady as a ruled line. "Ask me the right question. Not who he agreed with. Ask what happened the night he stopped agreeing."
