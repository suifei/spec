# Chapter One — Landfall

The ferryman would not step onto the Halvis jetty. He held the boat against the pilings with a boathook, waited for Lin to haul the two crates ashore, and shoved off before she had straightened her back. "Light's yours now," he called, already small on the grey water. "Mind you keep it. And mind you don't answer it."

Lin did not ask what that meant. She had learned, in the weeks of getting this posting, that Halvis people answered questions with the backs of their heads.

The lighthouse stood at the island's north horn, white gone the colour of old teeth. The keeper's cottage crouched at its foot. On the cottage door someone had nailed a strip of tin, and scratched into it, recently — the scratches were bright — three words: *Aldous kept watch.*

Aldous Vane. The keeper before her. The one nobody on the mainland would describe except to say he was **gone** — not dead, they were careful, just gone, the way you'd say of a tide. Six weeks now. His clothes still hung in the cottage press. His tea had gone to a green crust in the cup.

That first night Lin climbed the tower to light the lamp, and found it warm. Not lit — but warm, the great brass housing holding a heat no six-week-cold lighthouse should hold. She put her palm flat against it and felt, faintly, the memory of a flame.

She went down and found the log, and read the last line Aldous Vane had ever written, in a hand steady as a ruled line: *No light tonight. Weather clear. Keeping it dark, as agreed.*

As agreed. She read it three times. The lamp had been lit that night — the whole coast had seen it, the ferryman said so, that was the one thing anyone would say. And the keeper's own hand swore he had kept it dark.
